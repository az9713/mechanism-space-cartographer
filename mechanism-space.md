## 1. System Model

**Business:** Small web design agency. 3 designers, 1 owner/salesperson. Inbound-only.

**Actors:**
- Owner (sole salesperson, proposal writer, account manager)
- 3 designers (named in proposals, do delivery)
- Prospect (solo business owner or senior employee with authority to sign)

**Workflow states:**

```
Lead contacts
  -> [1-7 day lag] -> Discovery call
  -> [1-2 days]   -> Proposal sent
  -> [variable]   -> Decision: close / ghost / "too expensive" / competitor / "revisit later"
```

**Key bottlenecks:**
- 1-7 day lag to first call: competitors may be contacted first
- Proposals sent cold by email with no scheduled review: die in inboxes
- No budget qualification before proposal: some proposals are fundamentally price-misaligned
- 6 of 8 proposals lost monthly = $69,000/month in proposals that don't close
- Loss modes are roughly equal — ghost, price, competitor, deferral — no single dominant cause

## 2. Economic Equation

```
Monthly Revenue = Leads x P(proposal sent) x P(close) x Avg Project Value
               = 12 x 0.67 x 0.25 x $11,500
               = $23,000/month

If P(close) -> 0.375 (+50% relative):  Monthly Revenue = $34,500  (+$11,500/month)
If P(close) -> 0.50  (+100% relative): Monthly Revenue = $46,000  (+$23,000/month)
```

Lost deals are permanent. Every percentage point of close rate = ~$920/month.

## 4. Full Mechanism List

### Timing

1. **Speed-to-first-response** — Contact leads within 1 hour of inquiry. Every hour of lag cedes ground to competitors.
2. **Same-day discovery scheduling** — Reduce the 1-7 day lag to 24 hours maximum.
3. **Proposal within 4 hours of discovery call** — Prospect enthusiasm peaks immediately post-call; multi-day delay allows doubt to fill the gap.
4. **Proposal walkthrough call at send time** — Schedule a 20-minute review call at the moment of sending.
5. **Read-receipt follow-up** — If proposal software shows the prospect opened the proposal, call within 1 hour.
6. **Early morning send** — Send proposals and follow-ups at 7-8am prospect time.
7. **Avoid Friday sends** — Proposals sent Friday compete with the weekend mental reset and a full Monday agenda.
8. **14-day proposal expiry** — Explicit expiry date converts "revisit later" into a real decision point.
9. **Decision-window alignment** — Ask during discovery: "When do you need to decide?" Time all follow-ups around that date.
10. **Structured follow-up cadence** — Email day 1, email day 3, call day 7, close-or-kill day 14. Daily calls signal desperation.
11. **Re-engagement with date commitment** — When told "revisit later," get a specific date and calendar invite.
12. **30-minute response window** — Be reachable by phone/text within 30 minutes while a proposal is outstanding.
13. **End-of-month timing** — Push final follow-ups in the last week of the month when business owners plan ahead.
14. **Expiry extension offer** — When an expired proposal stalls, proactively extend; removes re-engagement awkwardness.
15. **48-hour cooling-off** — Don't contact for 48 hours after sending; immediate follow-up can signal desperation.

### Sequencing

1. **Budget qualification before discovery call** — Ask budget range before scheduling; surfaces mismatches before proposal investment.
2. **Verbal close before written proposal** — End discovery with: "If the proposal reflects what we discussed, are you ready to move forward?"
3. **ROI conversation before price reveal** — 10 minutes calculating the business value of a better site before any pricing discussion.
4. **Problem statement confirmation** — Summarize what you heard before discussing solutions: "Have I understood your situation correctly?"
5. **Post-discovery summary email** — Brief summary before the formal proposal; eliminates surprise and surfaces misalignments early.
6. **Objection surfacing in discovery** — Ask: "What would make you hesitate even if the proposal looks right?" Address it in the proposal.
7. **Timeline confirmation before proposal** — Confirm you can hit their deadline before writing.
8. **Decision-maker confirmation** — Confirm "Are you the person who signs off?" during discovery.
9. **Price range signal during discovery** — Give a ballpark range before writing the full proposal.
10. **"What does success look like?" framing** — Use the prospect's exact language about success in the proposal.
11. **Competitor awareness early** — Ask "Have you looked at other agencies?" and address comparisons in the proposal.
12. **Pre-call agenda 24 hours before discovery** — Prepared prospects engage more deeply and share more.
13. **Next steps agreed verbally during discovery** — "Proposal by [date], review call on [date+1]." Reduces ghosting.
14. **Proposal walkthrough call instead of email send** — Walk through live; objections surface and close in real time.
15. **Micro-commitment at end of discovery** — "Does this sound like the kind of project you want to move forward on?"

### Pricing & value framing

1. **Three-tier proposal** — Essential / Recommended / Premium at $8k / $11.5k / $15k+; anchoring makes the middle choice feel reasonable.
2. **Value-before-price proposal structure** — Business outcome before pricing section; price anchors against value, not budget ceiling.
3. **ROI calculator** — "If the new site converts 1 extra customer/month at $2,500 average, it pays for itself in 5 months."
4. **Payment plan option** — 3 x $3,833 alongside lump sum; removes budget as a barrier.
5. **Total cost of delay framing** — "Every month at your current site is costing approximately $X in lost leads."
6. **Current site audit in proposal** — Brief audit of what's broken and its likely revenue impact.
7. **Competitor price anchoring** — Larger agencies charge $25k-$50k; contextualizes $11,500 as good value.
8. **Scope visualization** — Break down what $11,500 buys; itemization makes price feel earned, not arbitrary.
9. **Decoy pricing** — Include a tier designed to make the target tier feel obviously right.
10. **Fast-start discount** — 5-10% off if signed within 5 days; economic urgency more credible than arbitrary deadlines.
11. **Price range instead of fixed** — "$10,000-$12,500" feels more consultative than a single number.
12. **Annual maintenance framing** — Mention optional maintenance after project cost; reduces post-launch abandonment fear.
13. **Peer ROI testimonial** — Quote from a past client about financial return.
14. **Budget question as pre-qualifier** — Ask budget directly in discovery; filters mismatches before proposal investment.
15. **Deposit reduction to commitment fee** — Small upfront "start fee" lowers the psychological barrier to the first yes.

### Behavioral / psychological

1. **Micro-commitment during discovery** — Verbal yes-in-principle before sending; small commitments predict follow-through.
2. **Reciprocity opener** — Send a useful quick-win resource before the discovery call.
3. **Named project framing** — Call it "The [BusinessName] Growth Project"; endowment effect creates psychological ownership before signing.
4. **Future pacing** — Describe the launch in present tense: "When your new site goes live in March, visitors will experience..."
5. **Scarcity signal** — "We have capacity for one more project starting in [month]" (must be real).
6. **Loss framing in follow-up** — "Every month at [current site] is a month of leads going to [competitor]."
7. **Social proof of peer behavior** — "We're working with 3 other [industry] businesses in [city] on similar projects."
8. **Status elevation** — Position clients as successful businesses; prospects want to be part of a successful peer group.
9. **Decision simplification** — One CTA: "Click here to approve and we'll start within 48 hours."
10. **Foot-in-the-door: free mini-audit** — Quick site audit during discovery; small yes creates consistency pressure.
11. **Post-discovery thank-you note** — Handwritten note after discovery; memorable in a digital world.
12. **Ask for the business explicitly** — "Are you ready to move forward?" Many salespeople never ask directly.
13. **Competitive shopping acknowledgment** — "I assume you're talking to other agencies — happy to answer questions that help you compare."
14. **Authority signals in email signature** — Recognizable client names or awards in every email.
15. **Proposal page count reduction** — Maximum 5 pages; shorter proposals get read completely and decided on faster.

### Trust & social proof

1. **Industry-specific case studies** — 2-3 case studies from the same industry inside the proposal.
2. **Measurable outcome framing** — Replace portfolio images with outcome data: "Increased inquiry rate 40%."
3. **Video testimonials** — 60-second client videos; harder to fake, more emotionally resonant than text.
4. **Risk reversal guarantee** — "We don't stop until you're satisfied, up to N revisions included."
5. **Named designer credentials** — Each designer's past work relevant to this project alongside their name.
6. **Referral source acknowledgment** — Explicitly reference who referred them and what you did for that referrer.
7. **Client reference offer** — "Happy to connect you with a past client in your industry if helpful."
8. **Logo wall** — Logos of recognizable local businesses served.
9. **Process visualization** — Visual of discovery -> wireframes -> design -> revisions -> handoff.
10. **LinkedIn connection before proposal** — Prospects check LinkedIn during decision-making.
11. **Response speed as trust signal** — Reply to all prospect emails within 2 hours.
12. **Third-party validation** — Design awards, platform partner status.
13. **Press or media mentions** — Third-party selection is a strong credibility signal.
14. **Live portfolio walk during discovery** — Screen-share portfolio; shared experience of quality beats a link.
15. **Professional, consistent team photos** — High-quality team section signals investment in craft.

### Friction removal + Selection

**Friction removal:**
1. **One-click e-sign flow** — Every additional step to say yes reduces close rate.
2. **Contract simplification** — 1-page plain-language contract; jargon creates anxiety and delay.
3. **Phone/text number at top of proposal** — "Questions? Text me: [number]"; prospects who can't easily ask often go silent.
4. **Interactive link instead of PDF** — Web-viewable proposal is mobile-friendly and easier to share.
5. **Payment at signature** — Embedded payment in the same tool as signature; separating them creates a second decision moment.

**Selection:**
6. **Lead scoring before proposal** — Score 1-5 before discovery; only write proposals for leads scoring 3+.
7. **Budget question on contact form** — Self-selection by price-aligned leads before they even call.
8. **Referral source tracking** — Track close rate by source; deprioritize low-close-rate sources.
9. **Industry fit filter** — Stop writing proposals for industries with historically poor close rates.
10. **Proposal investment qualifier** — "Our proposals take 2 hours to write — does $10k-$15k fit your range?"

## What to do this week

The top 5 mechanisms require zero new tools and zero budget:

1. Ask budget range before booking the discovery call
2. End every discovery call with "If the proposal looks right, are you ready to move forward?"
3. Ask "Does this sound like the kind of project you want to move forward on?" during the call
4. Reorder the proposal: outcome section before pricing section
5. Schedule a 20-minute review call at the moment you send every proposal

These five compound. A budget-qualified prospect who gave a verbal yes during discovery, received a value-first proposal, and has a review call booked is a fundamentally different situation than the current baseline.
